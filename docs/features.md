# Features

Every command, view, and setting in detail. The [README](../README.md) has the overview; the guide notes in your cabinet's `Guide/` folder walk through the same ground in the vault.

## Setup

The starter catalog includes ingredient descriptions, with optional **Family** and **Category** groupings where they help. A type can stand alone, and a family can have no category. The three default sugar syrups record ratios by weight and include preparation instructions. It supplies ingredient definitions and two bottle references, Angostura Bitters (Aromatic Bitters) and Peychaud's Bitters (Creole Bitters), with unknown availability. A new cabinet also gets a worked example: Limes and Lemons (availability), House Simple Syrup (1:1) (mL), and a Daiquiri and Whiskey Sour they complete, all with stock unknown. Setup adds the example only when it creates the cabinet note, so deleted examples stay deleted. See the [starter hierarchy](starter-hierarchy.md) for the complete list.

Setup creates ordinary Markdown notes and `.base` files (five resource views plus Related, Shopping list, In stock, and Restock) in a `Views` subfolder inside the chosen folder. The cabinet note explains your collection and the ingredient hierarchy, linking to each Base as it introduces the concept. Their tables reflect current notes rather than a snapshot of the starter catalog.

Existing starter notes are preserved, including custom descriptions and renamed pages that retain their catalog ID. Conflicting files or folders stop setup before any files are created. Setup is additive for notes and can finish an interrupted run; it does not overwrite or delete existing notes. The `.base` files in `<root>/Views/` belong to the plugin. Setup replaces any that differ from the current version, and on startup the plugin rewrites a view when a release or a setting changes it (it records the version it last wrote in its data, so column widths or sorting saved by Obsidian stay until then). **Refresh cabinet notes** rewrites every changed or missing view, restores deleted managed notes, and updates any you haven't edited. To keep a changed view, copy it into `<root>/Custom views/`, which setup creates and the plugin never touches. Keep catalog IDs on starter pages so later setup runs recognize them.

**Moving the cabinet.** Rename or move the cabinet folder, a folder inside it, or many cabinet notes at once, and the cabinet setting follows. Obsidian then rewrites every link to the moved notes, one note at a time, which can take a minute or two in a large cabinet. Until Obsidian restarts, the plugin changes no notes on its own (recipe estimates, rename following, view and catalog updates), since a write in the middle of Obsidian's rewriting can corrupt a note. On the next start it restores the cabinet's full-path links, which Obsidian rewrites in its own link format, and updates the views and any managed notes you haven't edited.

Setup also writes a guide in `<root>/Guide/`: Getting Started, Recipes, Stock, Making Drinks, Shopping, Hierarchy and Substitutes, and Views and Customizing, linked from the Cocktail Cabinet note, plus an **Agent Reference** for AI agents, which a vault's `AGENTS.md` can link instead of copying the cabinet's rules.

The Cocktail Cabinet and Shopping list notes and the guide are **managed notes**: the plugin keeps them current with each release, recording the version it last wrote in their `cc-managed` property. A managed note nobody has edited is replaced silently. One you've edited is left alone and offered in **Review cabinet updates**, which compares your version with the new one and offers **Use new version** (optionally saving yours beside it as `<name> (your version).md`) or **Keep mine**, which removes `cc-managed` so the note is yours from then on. Deleting `cc-managed` yourself does the same. A managed note you delete stays deleted until **Refresh cabinet notes**.

Starter notes are yours, but the catalog keeps them current where you haven't changed them. It remembers every value each field has had (description, preparation, parent, provides, typical ABV, catch-all, sugar ratio; never stock or prices), so when a release changes the catalog, a field still matching one of its values is updated silently. A field you've changed is offered in **Review cabinet updates** beside the catalog's new value, with **Use catalog's** or **Keep mine** (asked again only if the catalog changes it again), New starter notes in a release are added on startup, and a starter note you delete stays deleted. While anything waits for review, each startup shows a notice with a **Review** button, until every item is decided (**Skip all** declines the missing starter notes at once).

## Create and browse

- **Open cabinet**: open the Cocktail Cabinet note, with guidance and links to all five resource Bases. Ingredient types appear whether or not they have a family. Select a note link to open it, follow its classification links, or edit properties directly in a Base. Customize filtering, sorting, and columns using Obsidian. **Recipes** has a second view, **Latest**, sorted by creation date, newest first.
- **Add ingredient type**: create an ingredient with an optional family, description, and preparation instructions. Its **Level** choice adds a family or category instead, for optional groupings; switching keeps the name and description. Families and categories share the **Ingredient families and categories** Base, a view each. For a type, **Then add** (A bottle or Other stock) opens that form next, with the new type selected, for when you want both at once.
- **Add a bottle**: select its optional ingredient type (or create one with **New ingredient type** in the picker, when other bottles could fill the same role) and enter full bottle volume and ABV. Choose **Amount (mL)** to record what is left, or **Availability** to mark whether you have it. Bottles default to volume tracking and 100% full once bottle volume is entered. Estimate an existing bottle as percent full or enter mL directly; the form shows the resulting mL. A blank amount means none open. Leave unopened bottles blank if you have none. One note tracks a named bottle and its stock, including unopened bottles of the same size and strength.
- **Add other stock**: record fresh ingredients, prepared or bottled juice, homemade or purchased syrup, and other sources. Select the ingredient types they provide; if one doesn't exist yet, choose **New ingredient type** in the picker, which creates it and returns with it selected. Choose **Availability** (Unknown, Have, Low, or Out) or **Amount (mL)**. Other stock starts with **Availability** selected. One source can provide several ingredients. For **Homemade syrup** and **Prepared juice**, the name defaults to “House <type>”, such as House Simple Syrup (1:1), until you type your own.
- **Kitchen staples**: an everyday ingredient, such as salt or mint, recorded with only a name and availability, which recipes link directly. In **Add other stock**, choose the **Kitchen staple** form, which hides **Provides ingredients**.
- **Add a bottle of this type** and **Add other stock for this type**: on a type note, open the matching form with the type already selected.
- **Add recipe**: opens on the recipe form. Fill it in by hand, or paste copied recipe text, or a recipe page's address on its own, with two buttons at the top. **Paste a recipe…** takes copied recipe text, and **Read recipe** fills the form (replacing what's in it). **Read recipe page…** asks only for a page's address (`https://` optional) and fills the form from that page. Or type the address into **Source** and select **Read page**, which appears once Source holds a web address. A lone address pasted as recipe text is read as a page too. If a page can't be read, the paste dialog says why, with the address as a source line to paste the recipe under. The save button stays at the bottom of the window as the form scrolls, and **Description** folds away when empty. A page is read from its recipe data when it has some, else from its visible text; its address, without tracking parameters, becomes the source, and **Original text** shows what was read. Pages behind a sign-in or built by scripts can't be read: copy the text instead. Review the name, source, ingredients, method, and notes before saving. Typing an ingredient drops down matching ingredient notes (fuzzy search of names, filenames, aliases, and kind); arrow keys and Enter link one, shown by the note's name, and anything not picked stays plain text. Suggestions under each row match names, filenames, and aliases; select one to link it. The **Link text** setting decides the link's text: by default the cabinet name when the wording is the same name written differently (`lime juice` → Lime Juice), linking only that name when the wording has other words (`fresh [[…|Lime Juice]]`, `strawberry-infused [[…|Campari]]`), and the recipe's wording otherwise. **Keep the recipe's wording** turns this off; **Same name or an alias** also uses the cabinet name for an alias (`Strega` → Liquore Strega). Words are never dropped. The same rule picks the starting link text in **Review unlinked ingredients**, **Link this ingredient line**, **Link…** in logging and **Can I make this?**, and the `link-line` command, and you can choose other text there. Or select **Leave unlinked** to dismiss the suggestions for that row. A linked row whose text isn't the cabinet name gets a **Use "…"** button that switches it to the name; when the text contains the name plus other words (`bitter Amaro Campari`), **Move words out** links only the name and keeps the words beside it. Ignore them to keep the text. Each row is one line: amount, ingredient, a ⋯ menu, and remove. The menu has **Select link…** to search ingredient notes, **Create** items for types, bottles, other stock, families, and categories (without losing the recipe draft), **Unlink**, **Move up**, **Move down**, and **Add ingredient below**. A recognized volume shows in the amount's tooltip; only an amount logging can't read gets a note under the row. New ingredient notes are saved when their own creation form is submitted, even if the recipe is later canceled. Source wording and amounts are preserved, except that with **Fraction characters** on (the default) fluid-ounce amounts are written with fraction characters as they're read or typed: `1 1/2 oz`, `1 ½ oz`, and `1.5 oz` become `1½ oz`, and `.75 oz` becomes `¾ oz`. mL, ranges, and decimals with no exact fraction stay as written, and a saved amount changes only when you edit it; the creation date is resolved locally. The review also has a **Rating** and **Tags**. Tags are yours: the plugin defines none, and offers a button for each tag already on one of your recipes, plus a field to add another. They're saved in the note's `tags` property, so Obsidian's tag pane, search, and Bases see them. If you'd rather keep cocktail tags apart from the rest of your vault, nest them, such as `#cocktail/tiki`. Tags on the last line of a pasted or enhanced recipe's Tasting Notes move into the property. The form doesn't edit Tasting Notes: any in the source are kept as written. Impressions that change from one drink to the next fit better in the daily note: write them in that day's daily note, under the line logging adds. If the name matches a recipe you already have, ignoring accents, case, punctuation, “&”, and a leading “The”, or matches one of its aliases, a warning links to it.
- **Convert note to recipe**: open a Markdown page that isn't a cabinet note yet (it has no `cc-kind`) and run this command to review its ingredients, links, and method. Save to enhance that page, preserving existing properties (including tags, aliases, rating, and creation date). Pages outside the cabinet move into its Recipes folder, keeping their filename; the destination is shown before saving. Pages already inside the cabinet keep their location. Conflicting filenames or edits made since review opened stop the save.
- **Rename cabinet note**: from any cabinet note, give it a new proper name. The filename, heading, `cc-name`, and aliases follow, and links from other notes keep working; link text that showed the old name shows the new one, while other wording, such as a recipe's "lemon juice", stays. Renaming a cabinet note's file in Obsidian (its title or the file explorer) does the same once Obsidian has updated the links: `cc-name` becomes the new filename, the heading and aliases follow, link text showing the old name changes, and links in cabinet notes keep their full paths. A filename can't hold every character, so for a name such as Tuxedo #2 use **Rename cabinet note**.
- **Edit cabinet note**: from a recipe, bottle, other-stock, type, or family note, change what its creation form set, apart from the name and stock. Recipes: source, rating, tags, ingredients and their links, method, and description; saving moves tags at the end of Tasting Notes into the `tags` property; other text in the note stays as it is. Bottles: type, size, and ABV. Other stock: the types it provides and its form. Both pickers can create a new type. Types: family, shopping list, and typical ABV. Families: category. Changes made to the note after the form opens stop the save. On a recipe with lines **Tidy ingredient lines** would change on its own, the form says how many, with a button to open it (closing the form without saving).
- **Edit stock**: from a bottle or other-stock note, update what you have. **Bought more** adds a sealed bottle (or marks it Have) and takes it off the shopping list; **Finished** empties the open bottle (or marks it Out). You can also set the level as percent full or mL, the price, or switch between mL and availability tracking. Changes made to the note after the form opens stop the save.
- **Restock**: a view, linked from the Cocktail Cabinet note and the Shopping list, for picking what to buy. Running low means the last bottle at or under 10% full, or 70 mL without a bottle size and for other stock; both are settings. Its Running low, Out, and Unknown tables each have a **Shopping list** checkbox. Running low and Out list the most recently used first.
- **In stock**: everything on hand, bottles and other stock together. Its **Least used** tab puts what's been sitting longest first, never-used at the top, so you remember to use it.
- **Record purchases**: after shopping, the cart button in the Shopping list note's header (or the command, from anywhere). Check what you bought, one line per item: bottles get sealed bottles added, other stock in mL gets the amount added, availability-tracked stock becomes Have. Update the price, or the bottle size when it changed (750 to 700 mL); a different size such as 375 mL or 1 L is a separate bottle. For a type, pick which bottle, or add a new one. **Add something else…** covers things that weren't on the list, including new bottles. Bought items leave the list; the rest stay. Each purchase records `cc-last-purchased`, as does **Bought more** in Edit stock.
- **Review unlinked ingredients**: a tab listing measured recipe lines without a link, which logging can't subtract, grouped by wording ("gin: 16 recipes"), each card showing a few of its lines so what the wording means is plain. Pick what a wording links to from the suggestions, **Another note…**, or **New note…**; then every line is listed with a checkbox to untick any where it means something else, as it will read. A bar beside **Link** says what it will do, and **Link shows** picks the link's text: **As written** (each recipe's own wording), the note's name, or your own, starting on what the **Link text** setting chose. **Link** writes it into each recipe, keeping the amount. Select a recipe's name to open it beside the tab; the refresh button reads the recipes again. For things you don't buy, such as water, create a type tracked by availability and leave it at Have: logging passes over it, and the line counts as linked.
- **Insert ingredient link**: while editing a recipe, pick an ingredient note (a bottle, other stock, type, family, or category) to insert a link in the cabinet's style, `[[Cocktails/Types/Gin|Gin]]`. With text selected, it links the selection and lets you keep that text or use the cabinet name.
- **Link this ingredient line**: on an unlinked line in a recipe's Ingredients, link its wording. Pick an ingredient note, or create one named from the wording.
- **Edit this ingredient link**: with the cursor in a link in a recipe's Ingredients, change just that link (its note or its text); other links on the line stay. In Live Preview, clicking a link opens it, so click just after it and press ←.
- **Use cabinet name**: with the cursor in an Ingredients link whose text isn't the cabinet name, switch just that link's text to the cabinet name (dropping any other words in it) and give a short link its full path. It writes through the editor, so undo works. Also in the editor's context menu.
- These commands are also in the editor's right-click menu in recipes, each only where it applies. As commands, they can go on the mobile editing toolbar.
- **Log this cocktail**: from a cabinet recipe, review servings, what you used, how much will be left, and the proposed daily entry, then select **Log**. See the logging details below.
- **Undo last log**: put back what the latest logged drink changed; listed only while there's a log to undo. See [undoing a log](#undoing-a-log).
- **Tidy ingredient lines**: on a recipe note (command palette, file menu, or More options), list each Ingredients line that could be written in the preferred form, before and after, with a checkbox for each change: fluid-ounce amounts in fraction characters, short links given their full path, link text that the **Link text** setting counts as the same name switched to the cabinet name (these start ticked), and, unticked, link text replaced by the cabinet name (`Falernum` → Falernum Syrup) or extra words moved out of the link (`[[…|bitter Amaro Campari]]` → `bitter Amaro [[…|Campari]]`). **Tidy** writes the ticked changes; if the recipe changed since the list was made, nothing is saved.
- **Log a one-off**, **Riff on this cocktail**, and **Save one-off as recipe**: log a drink without a recipe note, and keep it as a recipe later. See [one-off drinks](#one-off-drinks).
- **Navigation in each note**: category, family, type, bottle, and other-stock notes embed sections from the shared `Views/Related.base`: a category's families; a family's types; the bottles and other stock under a family or type, with their stock; other stock of the same type on a bottle; and related recipes on all of them. Recipes count every link in the recipe note, followed up the hierarchy, so a family lists recipes that call for any of its types or bottles. They are grouped into recipes that call for the note (or something under it) and recipes it can fill through a broader requirement, each showing its rating as stars, highest first. These are core Bases, so they render with Cocktail Cabinet disabled. `Related.base` belongs to the plugin like the other views. Sort or column changes made in an embed apply to every note. The plugin keeps the sections present: when a release adds one, or a type gains subtypes, it adds the missing embed to each note beside its neighbors, without removing or moving the embeds already there. **Refresh cabinet notes** runs the same check.
- **Note actions**: commands about one note are also in that note's file menu: right-click it in the file explorer or its tab, or open the note's **More options** menu (⋯), which is the easiest way on mobile. Recipes also get a **Log this cocktail** button in the note's header, bottles and other stock an **Edit stock** button, and every note with editable fields (recipes, bottles, other stock, types, families) an **Edit cabinet note** button.

A recipe may reference **Rum**, **Aged Jamaican Rum**, or a particular bottle. Broad references stay broad. A narrow reference does not acquire every other type in the same family. Recipe text is not rewritten by navigation.

Recipe review accepts plain ingredient lines copied from a website, with optional bullets or Markdown headings. It recognizes familiar leading amounts, parenthetical brands, and a following preparation paragraph starting with a verb such as Stir or Shake. Structured pages can use Ingredients, Method (or Directions/Instructions), Description (or Notes), and Tasting Notes headings. Unknown sections and ingredient continuations are kept in Description with a review message. Existing wiki links stay as written; accepted suggestions add path-qualified links that display the recipe's wording. A generic ingredient is never automatically changed to a stocked bottle or a narrower type.

Amounts remain ordinary Markdown text. Single volumes in mL, cL, and US fluid ounces, including decimal, fractional, and mixed-number amounts, show a volume interpretation during review. When a line gives both ounces and mL, such as “2 oz (60 ml)” or “2 oz / 60 ml”, the **Amounts given in oz and mL** setting picks which printed amount counts: US fluid ounces by default, or mL. Prefer these for measured pours, but retain dashes, garnishes, ranges, and other source wording when appropriate. An explicit source measurement such as “1 barspoon (1/8 oz)” uses the stated 1/8 oz; a bare barspoon or dash has no assumed volume. Unclear amounts remain text and appear under **Not subtracted** during logging. No stock is deducted during recipe creation.

Notes, properties, links, and Bases are the durable system. They remain usable with Cocktail Cabinet disabled; live tables need only Obsidian's core Bases plugin. With Bases disabled too, the Markdown notes and links are still editable and navigable. The plugin adds setup, validated creation forms with ingredient selection, and hierarchy-aware related-recipe discovery. Future custom UI should have a specific workflow benefit beyond Obsidian's built-in note editing and Bases.

Each Base includes Markdown notes anywhere under the selected cabinet root with the corresponding `cc-kind`, including custom and renamed notes. It does not require a catalog ID or a complete parent chain. Notes elsewhere in the vault and other resource kinds are excluded. The tables show tracking modes, stock quantities in mL, manual availability, and purchasing fields. Bottle ABV remains a fraction (0.4 means 40%). No Dataview or Cocktail Cabinet code is needed to render these views. See [Obsidian's Bases syntax](https://obsidian.md/help/bases/syntax) and [embedding instructions](https://obsidian.md/help/bases/create-base).

New notes use plain Latin filenames derived from their proper name (Tuxedo #2 → `Tuxedo No 2.md`, Last Wørd → `Last Woerd.md`) and retain the proper name in `cc-name` and the page heading. Generated links display that proper name, for example `[[Cocktails/Families/Cachaca|Cachaça]]`. Accented names also become Obsidian aliases; the plugin's ingredient picker includes both spellings when they differ. Existing notes without `cc-name` use their filename as the displayed name.

Types, bottles, and other stock can be marked **Add to shopping list** independently of stock level. Bottles and other stock also accept an optional nonnegative **Last purchase price**, without a currency symbol. These fields are editable in the resource Bases. The **Shopping list** note, linked from the Cocktail Cabinet note, has a section each for flagged bottles (type, stock, last price), types (which bottles of that type you have and which aren't in stock), and other stock. Its `Views/Shopping list.base` belongs to the plugin like the other views.

## What can I make?

- **What can I make?** opens a tab with every recipe by how close it is: Can make, With a substitute, Check stock, Missing one, and Missing more, each with caveats such as “Low: Campari”. Filter by an ingredient, rating, or tag; within a group, the highest-rated recipes you haven't made lately come first.
- **What should I buy?** lists missing ingredients by the recipes each would unlock, with a button to add one to the shopping list.
- In both tabs, a recipe or note you select opens in a split beside the tab, reused for the next one; Ctrl or Cmd opens a new tab instead, and Ctrl or Cmd hover previews it.
- **What can I make with this?** on a bottle, other stock, type, or family opens the tab narrowed to recipes using it. **Can I make this?** on a recipe checks each line, with **Edit stock**, **Link…**, and **Log this cocktail** at hand.
- Agents can also add recipes through the command line: `cocktail-cabinet:create-recipe` creates a recipe note from recipe text, linking only remembered wordings, and `cocktail-cabinet:link-line` links one of its ingredient lines. Both write, so an agent's tool asks before each.
- Agents ask the same questions through the `obsidian` command line: `cocktail-cabinet:can-make`, `cocktail-cabinet:makeable`, and `cocktail-cabinet:to-buy` print JSON. See [availability](availability.md).

## Log a cocktail

Open a cabinet recipe and run **Cocktail Cabinet: Log this cocktail**.

- **Used:** each measured, linked ingredient gets a picker listing matching bottles and other stock you track in mL. A single match, or a named bottle you have, is selected for you; otherwise select one. A **catch-all type** (Amaro, Specialty Bitters; a checkbox on the type) lists its other bottles under the named one as likely substitutes, never selected for you. **Other bottle…** searches the whole cabinet. **Don’t subtract** skips the line; it is the default when nothing in your cabinet stocks that ingredient, such as fresh juice.
- **Stock:** what each bottle loses and has left. The open bottle is used first, then unopened bottles. A shortfall blocks logging; that bottle gets an **Edit stock** button, and every row has a gauge button, so you can fix stock that's out of date without leaving the log. Saving it refreshes the preview and keeps your choices.
- **Not subtracted:** lines that are unlinked, unmeasured, or not tracked in mL. A measured line without a link, or with a link to a missing note, gets a **Link…** button: pick a suggested ingredient note, search them, or create one. Choose the link text (the recipe's wording, the cabinet name, or your own) and the link is written into the recipe right away; the line moves up to **Used**; your other choices stay.
- **Usage:** logging records the log date (today unless you change it) as **Last used** on each bottle and other stock you used, including ones it doesn't subtract from (limes you track as Have, dashes of bitters), and **Last made** and **Times made** on the recipe.
- **Date:** defaults to today. If you forgot to log yesterday’s drink, pick that day; the daily entry goes in that day’s note, and **Last used** and **Last made** are only ever moved forward, so an older date never overwrites a later one. Future dates aren’t allowed.
- **Add to daily note:** appends “Made an Old Fashioned.” (or “Made 2 servings of …”) to the note for the chosen date, using the Daily notes core plugin’s folder, format, and template. Off by default if that day’s note already has this cocktail.

- **Alcohol:** above the stock table, an estimate for the bottles you've selected, such as “≈ 1.6 drinks (28.4 mL alcohol), 18.0% ABV · typical ABVs”, scaled by servings. Change a bottle to see what it does; cancel to log nothing.
- **Alcohol in the daily note** (off by default; turn on **Record alcohol in daily notes** in settings): when the log adds to the daily note, the “Made …” line ends with the estimate, as in “Made an Old Fashioned (≈ 1.6 drinks).”, and the estimate's mL of alcohol is added to the note's `cc-alcohol-ml` property, a running total for the day that a Base over your daily notes can sum by week. A one-off's header ends the same way, as in “Made a one-off (Rye thing) (≈ 1.6 drinks):”, and **Save one-off as recipe** keeps the drinks on the line. When some ABVs or amounts are unknown, the line says “≥” and the total grows by that lower bound.

### Undoing a log

Logged the wrong servings or the wrong bottle? Select **Undo** in the notice after logging, or run **Cocktail Cabinet: Undo last log**. It lists what it will put back: each bottle's stock and **Last used**, the recipe's **Last made** and **Times made**, the daily-note entry, and the daily alcohol total. Run it again to undo the log before that; the last 10 logs are kept. If a log stopped partway, undo puts back what it saved.

Undo won't touch stock that changed after the log, from another log or **Edit stock**: it refuses, and you fix that stock by hand. A date or count that changed since is left as it is. A daily note the log created stays, minus its entry.

### One-off drinks

For a drink with no recipe note, run **Cocktail Cabinet: Log a one-off**, or **Riff on this cocktail** on a recipe to start from its ingredients. Enter the ingredients per serving, linking what you poured as you type, then an optional name, and select **Review** for the same dialog as above (**Edit ingredients** goes back). No note is created: the daily note gets “Made a one-off (Name):” with the ingredients listed under it. If it's a keeper, open that daily note and run **Save one-off as recipe**, which is listed only on daily notes, or right-click the one-off's line to save that one: the recipe review opens with the name and ingredients filled in, and once saved the daily line links the new recipe, which counts as made that day.

If your stock or the recipe changed since the preview, nothing is saved and the preview refreshes. If saving fails partway, the message says what was already updated so the drink isn’t subtracted twice.

## Alcohol estimates

Each recipe gets an estimate of one serving's alcohol, ABV as served, and volume, which the plugin keeps current in the recipe's `cc-est-` properties. The **Recipes** view shows Drinks, Alcohol (mL), ABV, and Estimate columns, and its **Lower alcohol** view lists recipes from the least alcohol up.

- Each ingredient's ABV comes from the bottle the recipe names; otherwise the median of your in-stock bottles of that type; otherwise the type's **Typical ABV** (set it in **Edit cabinet note**, for types you don't have on hand); otherwise the median of bottles of that type you've had. A line naming a family, such as “rum”, uses your bottles in that family. Juices, syrups, and mixers in the starter are 0.
- Chilling adds water: by default 10% of the pour for built drinks, 18% stirred, 25% shaken, and 30% blended. The Method text decides which; otherwise a drink with juice, dairy, or egg counts as shaken. A recipe's `cc-dilution` property overrides it.
- A dash counts as 0.92 mL, a range as its midpoint, and a “top with” line without an amount as 60 mL. Garnishes and rinses don't count.
- **Estimate** says how sure it is: exact (named bottles), bottles (your in-stock bottles), typical (type values), past (bottles you've had), rough (a range or assumed top), or partial (an ingredient with no known ABV or amount; the figures are a minimum, shown with ≥, and the recipe stays out of Lower alcohol). The **Partial estimates** view in Recipes shows what keeps each partial estimate partial, from the recipe's `cc-est-missing`: a link for a note without an ABV, or plain text for a line to link or fix. The **Missing ABV** view in Bottles lists bottles without an ABV, in stock first.
- **Settings → Community plugins → Cocktail Cabinet** sets the standard drink (US 14 g by default, or UK units of 8 g), the dilution percentages, and the assumed top.

## Notes and properties

Notes use `cc-kind` to identify their role and `cc-`-prefixed properties to avoid common vault property conflicts. Links include vault-relative folder paths. See the [resource schemas](note-properties.md) for each property's purpose, stored values, and current use, the [cabinet design](cabinet-design.md) for design decisions, and the [domain glossary](../GLOSSARY.md) for terminology.

The `cc-` property prefix stands for Cocktail Cabinet. Cabinet property panels allow modestly wider labels for longer fields, with a responsive width limit. New starter notes use catalog IDs such as `family/Aquavit` so they display as text rather than URL schemes.
