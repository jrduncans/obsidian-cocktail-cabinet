# Knowledge bottle coverage review

Reviewed 2026-10-31 against the working [starter catalog](../src/catalog/catalog.ts): **124 types, 26 families, 6 categories**. The follow-up review approved four starter additions and confirmed the omissions recorded below. Other classifications remain proposals; the personal vault has not been migrated. Product facts are sourced separately in the [primary-source research notes](knowledge-bottle-product-research.md); the classification and inclusion recommendations here are judgments for review.

## Findings

Follow-up decisions: **Contemporary Gin**, **Crème de Menthe**, **Vermouth Amaro**, and **Vanilla Syrup** are now included in the starter. **Swedish Punsch** is personal-vault only; **Sloe Gin** is omitted so users can choose its classification. **Coconut Rum** remains omitted, and the user does not endorse a shared type for Cut & Dry and Coco Atoll. Crème de Menthe uses Herbal Liqueur. French Brandy and the remaining proposals are undecided.

The live `knowledge` vault has **165 bottle notes using 94 distinct types**, with 107 type notes in total. All 165 bottle notes are accounted for below, including out-of-stock and unknown-stock records. Six are syrup sources stored in the legacy Bottles folder. The comparison did not use quantities or prices.

| Result | Bottle notes | Meaning |
| --- | ---: | --- |

| Covered after a clear remapping | 34 | Existing starter type, with a naming change, a split of a catch-all, or a more appropriate existing type. |
| Additional type needed for the proposed mapping | 35 | 35 distinct proposed types absent from the starter. Most are candidates for local extensions, not 35 recommended starter additions. |

A same-name comparison alone would miss important cases: the old Amaro type contains several now-specialized styles; the old Falernum type means syrup; Laird’s bonded expression currently uses regular Apple Brandy; and St-Rémy currently uses Cognac. Conversely, Barbados Rum and Rhum Agricole Blanc already have reviewed starter counterparts with different names.

## Decisions from the first starter review

The initial seven-type shortlist covered **11 bottle notes**. Four types covering eight notes are now in the starter; Swedish Punsch and Sloe Gin were omitted, and French Brandy remains a proposal. Recipe counts are local evidence of demand, not a measure of how common an ingredient is generally. They count distinct recipes with resolved links in their Ingredients section to the corresponding legacy type or named bottles, without double-counting a recipe. A broad legacy type that splits across targets is excluded from the target's count. There are **501 recipe notes** in the live snapshot; unlinked requirements and mentions outside Ingredients are excluded.

The French Brandy count is **0**, not the 46 recipes linking the legacy Cognac type: those generic Cognac requirements cannot simply be reassigned to French Brandy. Its priority is classification accuracy, not demonstrated direct use of St-Rémy. The agreed starter definition of Brandy already supplies the appropriate family; the missing piece is a bottle-assignable type.

**Cocchi Americano Bianco deserves a separate reconsideration:** it has 11 named-bottle recipe references, but the earlier review deliberately omitted Quinquina coverage. A local **Bitter Aperitif Wine** type under a new **Aperitif Wine** family preserves the existing role without misclassifying it as Blanc Vermouth or Gentian Liqueur. If we restore this to the starter, define the recipe role and naming explicitly. Its producer describes an aromatized wine flavored with botanicals including gentian and cinchona. [Cocchi Americano](https://www.cocchi.it/en/wines/americano/).

The next candidates, if a wider starter is desired, are **Bergamot Liqueur, Blackberry Liqueur, Melon Liqueur, Pineapple Liqueur, Grapefruit Liqueur, Strawberry Liqueur**, and selected **Grapefruit, Mole, and Tiki Bitters**. Their counts and bottles appear below. They should not enter automatically simply because this collection contains them. Conversely, bottle count alone is too strict: Swedish Punsch has one bottle and 11 linked recipes; Crème de Menthe has one bottle and 11 linked recipes when bottle and type references are combined.

For the less familiar single-bottle flavors, these are identity-preserving proposals based on their existing type and product name, not claims of universally standardized categories. **Licor 43** and **Becherovka** are deliberately named types rather than newly invented “Other” buckets. Bénédictine already establishes that a named liqueur can be a type. The existing families are optional browsing groupings; a family match does not make these products alternatives for each other.

Specialty bitters account for **14** of the 35 gap bottles. Retaining each named flavor under the existing parentless **Bitters** family keeps them navigable without pretending that all satisfy Aromatic Bitters. For example, Bittercube describes Blackstrap with molasses and spices; sharing a seasoning format does not establish the same recipe role as Angostura. Scrappy’s Black Lemon can retain the legacy Lemon Bitters role if that breadth is intentional. Mole Bitters should retain its spiced profile rather than being silently absorbed into Chocolate Bitters. These are classification judgments supported by the distinct producer offerings. [Bittercube Blackstrap](https://bittercube.com/shop/products/blackstrap), [Scrappy’s Black Lemon](https://www.scrappysbitters.com/bitters/black-lemon-bitters), [Sazerac’s Bittermens portfolio](https://sazerac.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/231127-Sazerac-Portfolio2024.pdf).

Pineapple needs three identities: the existing **Pineapple Rum**, missing **Pineapple Liqueur**, and potentially a custom **Pineapple Amaro** if the broader Amaro type is too broad for Heirloom. Giffard explicitly describes Caribbean Pineapple as a liqueur, despite including aged rum. [Giffard Caribbean Pineapple](https://www.giffard.com/en/liqueurs-premium/4898-caribbean-pineapple-2.html).

## Existing coverage that needs explicit remapping

- **Amaro splits:** Campari and Crimson Snap → Red Bitter Liqueur; Aperol and Accompani Mari Gold → Orange Bitter Liqueur; Cynar → Artichoke Amaro; Fernet Branca → Fernet. Averna, Nonino, Montenegro, Lucano, Meletti, Amaro Di Angostura, and Amaro for Art have the general Amaro home. Heirloom is separately flagged for flavor-specific membership.
- **Other Herbal Liqueur splits:** Green Chartreuse, Flora Green, and Luxardo Del Santo → Green Herbal Liqueur; Yellow Chartreuse and Strega → Yellow Herbal Liqueur; Bénédictine → Bénédictine; Bitter Bianco → Gentian Liqueur as already agreed. Becherovka and Cascadia need the decisions recorded above. Do not preserve Other Herbal Liqueur as an extra shared type across the narrower groups.
- **Apple brandy:** Laird’s Straight Apple Brandy (Bottled in Bond) → Bonded Apple Brandy. Regular Apple Brandy remains a different type; Apple Brandy Family is the any-style requirement.
- **Falernum:** Portland Syrups Falernum and Latitude 29 Falernum → Falernum Syrup. Velvet Falernum and Avuá Falernum → Falernum. This reverses the legacy unqualified name without reversing ingredient meaning. The Portland producer explicitly identifies its product as syrup. [Portland Syrups](https://beveragemixers.com/products/falernum-syrup).
- **Rum:** Real McCoy 5 and Mount Gay Eclipse → Aged Barbados Rum; Don Q Cristal and Flor de Caña Extra Seco → Light Rum; Barbancourt 3 Star → Aged Haitian Rum; Clément Canne Bleue → Unaged Rhum Agricole; Paranubes → Unaged Mexican Rum; Mister Fogg → Blended Overproof Rum. These correspondences follow the [rum review](rum-organization-review.md), with the expression-level flags above retained.
- **Other names:** Cassis Liqueur → Blackcurrant Liqueur; PX Sherry → Pedro Ximénez Sherry; Classic Aromatic Bitters → Aromatic Bitters; New Orleans-Style Bitters → Creole Bitters. The last is a proposed naming correspondence supported by Scrappy’s own Creole description, rather than an additional required type. [Scrappy’s Orleans](https://www.scrappysbitters.com/bitters/orleans-bitters).
- **Concentration:** the bottle notes explicitly named Simple Syrup, 1 to 1 and Demerara Syrup, 2 to 1 fit the corresponding starter ratio types. The unspecific legacy type links in recipes still need a deliberate concentration decision; do not infer every recipe's ratio solely from the bottles currently in the vault.

## What is intentionally left outside the starter

**Pisco's omission is reaffirmed because of modeling complexity, not rarity.** The starter deliberately leaves users to choose a single Pisco type or their own family and subdivisions, accepting that someone adding a Pisco Sour immediately will need to create an ingredient type. The standalone Pisco proposal for Caravedo Torontel is one option for this personal vault, not a prescribed scheme for every user.

Existing agreed omissions affecting these bottles include **Pisco** (Caravedo Torontel), **Swedish Punsch**, and **Sloe Gin**, which can remain personal-vault types. **Coconut Rum** is also omitted: the user considers the name too broad to establish a shared recipe role for Malibu-style products, Planteray Cut & Dry, and Doctor Bird Coco Atoll. Preserve named-bottle requirements for the latter two; whether to use separate local types or no type, and how to handle the existing generic Coconut Rum requirement, remains open. **Quinquina/bitter aperitif wine** coverage was also deliberately omitted; Cocchi Americano is the concrete reason to reconsider it. This report does not reverse those decisions.

**Navy Strength Rum** was deliberately omitted as a separate type, but Mister Fogg is already covered by Blended Overproof Rum. Its classification is therefore not a remaining bottle gap. The FALSE IDOL recipe’s legacy Navy Strength requirement still needs an explicit recipe decision. Do not broaden that requirement simply by retiring its type.

A non-overproof **Unaged Jamaican Rum** type was deliberately omitted for lack of an encountered recipe need. If Hamilton Gold is confirmed to belong there, add that type locally or reconsider the omission deliberately. Other prior omissions, such as port styles, have no bottle note in this snapshot and were not tested by this collection.

The remaining local-extension recommendations are proposals, while Swedish Punsch, Sloe Gin, Coconut Rum, and Pisco have explicit starter-omission decisions.

## How this cabinet can work with the plugin

1. Create the starter definitions, then add approved local types under the proposed existing families. Only Bitter Aperitif Wine currently needs a proposed new family, Aperitif Wine. Standalone types such as Swedish Punsch, Honey Liqueur, and Pisco need no family just to be valid.
2. Adopt bottle records with links to these ingredient **types**, not their families. Assign only the types whose shared matching behavior is intended. The plugin's bottle form currently selects types; a family alone does not fill that field.
3. Preserve recipes that name bottles. For a generic type requirement, resolve the old type’s actual meaning, especially Falernum, cacao color, syrup strength, and the old Amaro/Other Herbal Liqueur catch-alls. A new shared type makes its bottles same-type candidates; this is a meaningful classification choice, not just organization.
4. Keep named products distinct from inventory format. The six syrup records can be other-stock sources providing their syrup types, even when commercially bottled, or remain bottles where bottle-specific stock tracking is useful. Their presence in the old Bottles folder does not require a new ingredient type. All six now have starter syrup types, including the newly added **Vanilla Syrup**.
5. Use ordinary notes, properties, links, and Bases for the resulting cabinet. The plugin supports custom types and optional parents already; these omissions do not require code support. Automated conversion of the old schema is still future work, so this is a classification plan rather than a completed migration.

The initial audit was read-only. The follow-up implemented the four approved starter additions and supplied their definitions to the dev cabinet; **knowledge, its inventory, and its recipes remain unchanged**. It does not add compatibility mappings for early plugin versions. Existing private-vault meanings are being reviewed separately so a future adoption preserves the collection intentionally.
